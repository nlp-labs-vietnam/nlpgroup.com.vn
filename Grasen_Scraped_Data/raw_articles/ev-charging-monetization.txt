EV Charging Monetization: 6 Ways Commercial Charging Stations Generate Revenue

EV charging infrastructure requires significant capital investment. For commercial operators, understanding how to generate recurring revenue is essential to achieving a viable business case. Here are six proven approaches to EV charging monetization.

1. Per-kWh Charging Fees

The most straightforward model: charge users per kilowatt-hour of energy delivered. This aligns cost directly with usage and is transparent for EV drivers.
- Set kWh rates above cost of electricity + overhead
- Apply time-of-use pricing (peak/off-peak) to shift demand
- Integrate with OCPP-compliant CSMS for automated billing
- Typical public DC fast charging: $0.30–$0.60/kWh in developed markets

2. Session and Time-Based Fees

Beyond kWh, operators can add:
- Session start fee: flat charge per charging event
- Idle/overtime fee: charged when a vehicle occupies a charger beyond its charging time
- Reservation fee: hold a charger for a defined time window
Grasen's CSMS supports all three fee layers simultaneously.

3. Subscription and Membership Plans

Offer EV drivers discounted rates in exchange for a monthly or annual membership fee:
- Predictable recurring revenue for operators
- Improved loyalty and repeat usage
- Tiered plans (basic/premium) can match different usage levels
- Common for fleet operators and corporate charging programs

4. Advertising Revenue (Digital Screens)

High-power DC chargers with large touchscreens (like Grasen's S 240 with its 55-inch advertising display) can generate ad revenue:
- Captive audience during charging sessions (15–40 min dwell time)
- Local business advertising, brand partnerships
- Content management via CSMS or dedicated ad platform
- Particularly effective at high-traffic highway locations and commercial districts

5. Fleet Charging Contracts

Offer dedicated charging capacity and priority service to fleet operators:
- Long-term contracts provide revenue certainty
- Fleet rates can be lower per-kWh but offset by volume and reliability premium
- Data reporting for fleet cost allocation
- Depot charging with shift scheduling reduces peak demand costs

6. White-Label CPO Software and Network Access Fees

Advanced operators and platform providers can monetize their CSMS:
- License the management platform to smaller operators
- Charge network access fees for using shared billing, roaming, and reporting infrastructure
- Enable interoperability with other charging networks via eMSP roaming agreements (e.g., OCPI protocol)

Maximizing Revenue: Practical Tips

- Location matters: High-traffic sites (highways, shopping centers, transit hubs) drive volume
- Uptime is revenue: Every hour of downtime loses charging sessions; target 95%+ uptime
- Dynamic pricing: Adjust rates by time-of-day and local demand to maximize yield
- Multiple revenue streams: Combine kWh fees + idle fees + advertising for maximum site ROI
- Software integration: A robust CSMS reduces manual overhead and enables automated pricing adjustments

Conclusion

A well-structured EV charging business can generate revenue through multiple channels. Combining per-kWh fees, session charges, subscriptions, advertising, fleet contracts, and platform licensing creates a resilient revenue model that can support a positive return on investment over the typical 10–15 year hardware lifecycle.
