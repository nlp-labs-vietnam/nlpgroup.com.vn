EV Charging Management Software: Managing a Charging Network from One Backend

As EV adoption grows, operators managing multiple charging stations increasingly need centralized tools to oversee their entire network. EV charging management software — also known as a Charging Station Management System (CSMS) — provides a single backend platform to monitor, control and optimize charging infrastructure at scale.

What Is EV Charging Management Software?

EV charging management software is a cloud-based or on-premise platform that connects to charging hardware via the Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP). It allows operators to:
- Monitor charger status in real time (available, charging, faulted, offline)
- Remotely start, stop, and reset charging sessions
- Manage user authentication via RFID cards or mobile apps
- Set and update pricing and billing configurations
- Generate usage and revenue reports
- Receive fault alerts and push firmware updates remotely

Key Features of a Good CSMS

1. Real-Time Monitoring
Operators can view every charger's status across all sites on a single dashboard. Alerts are triggered automatically for faults, communication losses, or abnormal power readings.

2. User Management
The system supports multi-tier access — operators, site managers, and end users each have different permission levels. RFID cards, mobile app authentication, and Plug & Charge (ISO 15118) are common access methods.

3. Dynamic Load Management (DLM)
DLM balances power distribution across multiple chargers on a shared electrical circuit, preventing overloads without requiring expensive grid upgrades.

4. Flexible Billing & Pricing
Operators can configure pricing by time-of-use, per kWh, per session, or flat subscription. Multi-currency and tax settings are supported for international deployments.

5. Payment Integration
The CSMS handles payment via integrated card readers, third-party payment gateways (e.g., Stripe, WeChat Pay, Alipay), or prepaid account balances. Transaction data is managed securely without storing full card details on hardware.

6. Multi-Site, Multi-Operator Support
Enterprise deployments require hierarchical access — a parent CPO managing multiple site operators. Role-based access controls allow granular permissions at each level.

7. Reporting & Analytics
Usage reports, energy consumed per session, revenue per site, and charger uptime statistics help operators optimize network performance and plan capacity.

OCPP Compliance

The Open Charge Point Protocol (OCPP) is the industry-standard communication protocol between chargers and the CSMS. OCPP 1.6J is the most widely deployed version, while OCPP 2.0.1 introduces enhanced security and smart charging capabilities. Grasen's CSMS is OCPP 1.6J compliant and supports integration with third-party management platforms.

Grasen CSMS Overview

Grasen's Charging Station Management System (CSMS) is designed for commercial and fleet operators. Key capabilities include:
- Multi-charger, multi-site dashboard
- Dynamic Load Management (DLM)
- RFID and mobile app authentication
- Three-phase billing: reservation fee, energy fee, overtime fee
- Secure payment processing
- Public-facing charging station promotion tools
- Remote diagnostics and firmware updates

The platform is available as a SaaS cloud service or can be deployed on-premises for operators with data sovereignty requirements.

Use Cases

- CPOs (Charge Point Operators): Public charging networks benefit from centralized billing, reporting, and user management.
- Fleet Operators: Depot charging management with shift-based scheduling and driver authentication.
- Commercial Sites: Hotels, retail centers, and office buildings use CSMS to offer employee or guest charging with cost allocation.
- Municipalities: Public charging infrastructure requires robust reporting and multi-operator access control.

Conclusion

EV charging management software is no longer optional for operators running more than a handful of chargers. A capable CSMS reduces operational complexity, improves uptime, and enables revenue optimization across an entire charging network from a single backend interface.
